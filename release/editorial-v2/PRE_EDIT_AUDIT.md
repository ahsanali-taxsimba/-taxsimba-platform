## Findings supported by the source

1. **A useful foundation, uneven completeness.** All 120 have source links and specific customer topics. Guides on cumulative reporting, keeping the two tax years separate and submission evidence address genuine confusion. But the first two cost articles mostly teach quote comparison, with no sourced market-price answer. A reader can still leave with the headline question unresolved.
2. **Repetition needs editing.** All 120 contain the exact headings “A decision guide for this situation”, “Two more questions you may have” and “Your preparation worksheet”. Four explanatory/caution paragraphs are also repeated across every article. Consistent components are not in themselves an SEO fault; forcing the same modules everywhere makes the writing feel formulaic and can duplicate useful material already explained.
3. **Broad titles exceed some base copy.** The original base bodies for the general Self Assessment, tax-saving tips and MTD explainer pages contain 258, 168 and 207 words respectively before additions. Short content is not inherently bad; the issue is that these broad promises need substantive, topic-specific answers rather than generic appended scenarios/checklists.
4. **Internal workflow terminology appears in customer advice.** Priority articles 3 and 8 use payment/dashboard unlock language; article 8 mentions admin approval. Replace this with what a customer needs to know: send the missing evidence, get any extra work and fee agreed, and obtain confirmation of the correction. Verify actual service capability before making product promises.
5. **Reviewer attribution is not evidence of a named professional review.** Eighty-two records have an empty reviewerName; 38 say TaxSimba. None of those fields identifies a particular qualified reviewer. Eighty-two records carry an internal verified-against-official-guidance status; that status must not be represented as independent approval.
6. **Sentences are mostly short, but that does not prove natural writing.** Median body length is 636 words (range 424–1,197). A simple prose-sentence heuristic gives a median per-article average of 12.8 words. These figures help find outliers; they are not validated reading-age scores. Jargon, repeated caveats and indirect answers still need an editor.
7. **Technical SEO and editorial competitiveness are separate.** Existing build/HTTP evidence remains useful. This pass does not invalidate working metadata, sitemap or assets. It does invalidate a blanket inference that engineering checks establish superior writing.

## Standard for simple, natural writing

Answer the title question in the first two or three sentences. Use everyday language, define unavoidable tax terms once, and give the reader a specific next action. Prefer one useful worked example to several descriptions of what an accountant might check. Remove clauses that merely repeat “check with your accountant”; retain qualifications that materially change the answer. Use topic-specific headings. Keep a table, FAQ or worksheet only where it helps that particular question. Do not add length for its own sake.

TaxSimba can describe the work as AI-assisted and edited/reviewed according to the actual process. Do not describe the drafts as human-authored. A real human review can be recorded when it happens, with the reviewer's consent, role and date. Plain English is a quality standard, not an authorship claim.

Illustrative language edits (not applied to the release):

- Instead of “A dashboard unlock is an administrative step, not an HMRC amendment in itself”, use: “Paying for a correction does not change HMRC's figures. Ask your accountant to confirm when the correction has been made.” Use this only where that payment workflow is actually relevant to the product.
- Instead of “The relevant authorisations must match the work your new accountant is taking over”, use: “Make sure your new accountant has HMRC permission for the jobs they will handle.”
- Instead of “An MTD price only makes sense when you know which jobs it covers”, use: “Before choosing an MTD plan, check whether the price covers software, bookkeeping, quarterly updates and your annual return.” The completed cost article still needs verified price information or a narrower title.

## Release acceptance criteria

1. Each article resolves its specific question, including the key number, deadline or eligibility rule where that is what the title promises. Necessary qualifications sit beside the answer.
2. Every material tax claim is tied to an applicable official source and the right tax year; examples are recalculated. Complex tax topics receive qualified review.
3. Each article offers a distinct benefit compared with a recorded sample of relevant current pages. Similar TaxSimba pages have separate intents and helpful cross-links. A similarity flag is a review prompt, not evidence of an actual ranking conflict.
4. A plain-English editor removes repetition and internal terminology and checks tone aloud. Intended readers can identify the answer and next action without needing an accountant to translate it.
5. All service/price/capability claims and SA/MTD CTAs match the live offer and tested customer journey. Articles explain when the reader may not need to buy a return.
6. A dated per-article review log records who checked what, any unresolved issue and the final copy/version. No “independently reviewed” label until a genuinely separate reviewer has done that work.
7. Rebuild and rerun the relevant article/link/mobile checks after content changes. Retain the 20-first order, 120 URLs and existing brand assets.
